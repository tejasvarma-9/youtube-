Rules that keep the fact-check short (breaking them is what causes repeated rewrites):
- Say a rate, margin or return as "a year" or "annualized" the first time it appears, so nobody reads it as a quarter's or a month's amount.
- Give an as-of date once, where the figure first appears. A recap repeats the figure, not the date, unless the figure is different.
- Never present the difference between two different things (a savings rate and a central bank rate, a price and a cost) as what a company keeps, pockets or earns. Describe each number on its own and say what it covers. Never call a margin, revenue or a gross figure "profit", "kept" or "pocketed": it is before costs.
- No sentence longer than 28 words, and at most two figures in any sentence. A recap or ending question never adds a number that has not already been said earlier.
- Do not add a sentence just to restate a fact; every sentence must add something. Reach the target length with new, sourced material (a second example, another country or year, what the number means for the viewer), not repetition.
- Use only the primary source (filing, regulator, official page) for every figure; never news coverage of it.

Facts rules: use only figures you found in Step 1, and cite them. Any other number must be spoken as an estimate ("roughly", "industry estimates suggest") and marked ESTIMATE. Never invent quotes.
