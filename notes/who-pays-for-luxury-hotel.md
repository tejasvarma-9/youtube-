Research brief: "Who Really Pays for a Luxury Hotel?" (written 2026-10-06)

HOW TO USE THIS FILE: use only the figures in section A and the quotes in section B. Section C is context the fact-checker must confirm before any line from it is used; if it cannot, cut it. Do not add any hotel figure that is not in this file. Do not name individual hotels or brands. Do not suggest any company behaves badly: describe how the contracts are written, in the companies' own words.

THE ANGLE (our read, say it once as "one way to read this"):
The big hotel brands mostly do not own the hotels that carry their names. Owners put up the building, take on the debt and pay the running costs. The brand collects fees that are charged on revenue, so they are owed whether or not the owner makes a profit. The brand is paid first; the owner is paid last.

OPENING IDEA (puzzle that withholds the answer, per voice.md): Marriott has about 1.8 million rooms. It says it owns or leases fewer than one in a hundred. So who paid for the rest, and why does Marriott still book billions in revenue? Ask the question inside the first 90 words, reveal the answer at about 60 to 90 seconds. No roadmap, no channel intro.

A. FIGURES CHECKED BY ME AGAINST THE PRIMARY DOCUMENT (US$ millions unless stated)
Marriott International, full year 2025 (earnings release filed 10 Feb 2026, sec.gov, 8-K Ex. 99):
- Franchise fees 3,325. Base management fees 1,322. Incentive management fees 791. Gross fee revenues 5,438.
- Owned, leased, and other revenue 1,679. Cost reimbursement revenue 19,204. Total revenues 26,186. Operating income 4,141.
- Our arithmetic (say "about"): gross fees are about 21% of total revenue; cost reimbursement revenue is about 73%; owned/leased/other is about 6%.
- Rooms at 31 Dec 2025: total system 1,779,936 (10-K). The 10-K groups franchised, licensed and other at 1,183,513 and company-operated at 580,170.
Hilton Worldwide, full year 2025 (earnings release 11 Feb 2026):
- Franchise and licensing fees 2,780. Base and other management fees 376. Incentive management fees 313. Ownership 1,233. Other revenues 252. Cost reimbursement revenues 7,085. Total revenues 12,039. Operating income 2,693.
- Our arithmetic: the three fee lines add to 3,469, about 29% of total revenue; cost reimbursement is about 59%; ownership is about 10%.
- Rooms at 31 Dec 2025 (10-K): ownership 15,287; managed 264,760; franchised/licensed 1,071,304 (this line includes timeshare in the 10-K's grouping). Our arithmetic: owned rooms are about 1% of the total.
Host Hotels & Resorts (a large hotel OWNER, 10-K for 2025). Say "one large owner describes its own contracts", not "the industry":
- Base management fee: "in most instances is calculated as a percentage (generally 2-3%) of annual gross revenues".
- Incentive management fee: "typically is calculated as a percentage (generally 10-20%) of operating profit".
- Management agreement initial terms: "range from 10 to 50 years".
- FF&E reserve: "typically 4-5%" of the hotel's gross revenues is set aside.

B. QUOTES CHECKED AGAINST THE FILINGS (use as written; attribute each to the company)
- Marriott 10-K: "Consistent with our focus on franchising, management, and licensing, we own or lease very few of our lodging properties (less than one percent of our system)."
- Marriott 10-K: "Under our hotel franchising arrangements, we generally receive an initial application fee and continuing royalty fees, which typically range from four to seven percent of room revenues, plus for certain brands, up to four percent of food and beverage revenues..."
- Marriott 10-K: "Under our management agreements, hotel owners are generally responsible for hotel operating costs, including centralized programs and services."
- Marriott 10-K: "Current and potential hotel owners must periodically spend money to fund new hotel investments, as well as to refurbish and improve existing hotels."
- Hilton 10-K: "In general, the owner pays all operating and other expenses and reimburses any of our out-of-pocket expenses."
- Hilton 10-K: management fees "generally consist of a base management fee, which is generally based on a percentage of the hotel's monthly gross operating revenue, and, when applicable, an incentive management fee, which is generally based on a percentage of the hotel's operating profits, normally over a one calendar year period, and, in some cases, may be subject to a stated return threshold to the hotel owner."
- Hilton 10-K: "Hotel owners with financial difficulties have been and may continue to be unable or unwilling to pay us amounts that we are entitled to under our existing contracts on a timely basis or at all."
- Host Hotels 10-K: "Expenditures for these major repairs and improvements affecting the hotel building typically are funded directly by owners but may also be funded from the FF&E reserve account."
Our own plain-English points that follow from these quotes (say them as our read, not as the company's words):
- A base fee is a percentage of revenue, so it is calculated before any profit is known. Contracts vary, so say "generally".
- Cost reimbursement revenue is the line Marriott and Hilton use for costs they get paid back for. The Marriott quote above says owners are generally responsible for those costs. We have NOT found the companies' exact definition of this line, so do not say "all of this comes from owners": say "the filings say owners are generally responsible for these costs".

C. CONTEXT THE FACT-CHECKER MUST CONFIRM BEFORE USE (read by a research agent, not re-checked by me)
- Marriott 10-K says its management agreements may be ended early by owners if Marriott does not meet certain performance metrics. Hilton says some of its management contracts give owners early termination rights if performance criteria are missed. These balance the story: owners are not powerless.
- Marriott makes loans to hotel owners and gives guarantees to owners or lenders (Marriott 10-K). Balance point: the brand carries some risk too.
- CBRE (US hotel industry benchmark, reported May 2026): 2025 US gross operating profit margin 34.8% and EBITDA margin 22.8%. Check the primary CBRE source before use. Do NOT claim this shows the owner's share after fees.
- Property improvement plans: Hilton 10-K says owners must satisfy plans "to keep the hotels in compliance with our brand standards". Confirm the exact wording. Do not use per-room renovation figures (the only ones found are from 2017).
- US industry occupancy for August 2026: 66.4%, average daily rate $161.78, revenue per available room $107.43 (CoStar STR). Secondary source, read through a mirror. Use only if confirmed on CoStar's own page. Do not use for the main story.
- Do NOT use: any figure for IHG or Hyatt, any claim about luxury-tier fee rates (the only figure found is from 2016), the "51 owners wrote to Marriott" report (second-hand, not read at source), any court case, any claim that Marriott cut loyalty charges.

D. WHAT THE FILINGS DO NOT SAY (the script must be honest about this)
- Neither company separates luxury hotels from the rest in these figures. The model described is system-wide. Say so once, plainly, and do not invent luxury-specific numbers.
- None of the filings states what share of a typical owner's profit the fees take. Do not make up a "who gets the dollar" split for a room.

E. ILLUSTRATION (allowed, label it "a rough illustration, not a real hotel"):
Take the range "2 to 3 percent of gross revenues" for a base management fee. On a hotel that takes in $100 million in a year, that is $2 million to $3 million to the manager, worked out before the owner pays staff, debt and repairs. Do not add a profit figure.
